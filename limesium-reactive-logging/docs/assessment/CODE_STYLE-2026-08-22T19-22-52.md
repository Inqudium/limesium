## 1. Executive summary

The module shares the servlet twin's style maturity and, being its deliberate copy in the contract layer, most of its strengths verbatim: ktlint enforced and green, `internal` on every collaborator, constructor injection, one `@ConfigurationProperties` data class with `require` invariants, `fun interface`s with companion defaults, `object`s for key holders, KDoc written as *why*. The reactive-specific code is idiomatic for its stack — decorators with a private top-level `tee` function, a `CoWebFilter` whose signal mapping is a plain `try`/`catch` with rethrow, `Mono.defer` plus `doOnError`/`doOnCancel`/`doFinally` rather than imperative bridging, a single `AtomicReference` state on `Exchange`. "Java in Kotlin" is rare: one `!!` (guarded), no `lateinit` in production code, no util class, `require`/`check` instead of manual throws.

The deviations cluster in the same three places as in the twin, but two of them are **larger here**. The Given/When/Then convention is missed entirely in 19 of 99 test methods — eight of the thirteen auto-configuration tests, three of the five `traceparent` tests, all four twin pins — and partially in 21 more; CLAUDE.md allows no exception. Inline fully-qualified names stand in for imports at roughly 24 sites in four test classes, with `java.time.Duration.ofMillis` and `reactor.core.publisher.Flux.just` spelled out while everything around them is imported. And 21 KDoc passages anchor a design decision to "assessment finding N, 2026-08-22 analysis", a pointer that no longer identifies one document. The emitter carries the same Java-style definite assignment before its classification `when` as the twin, the same `var` builder chain, and the same `!!`; two further small points are the file name `MdcKeys.kt` hiding `MdcScope`, and a charset lookup written twice with `runCatching`. No enforced rule is violated; nothing is actively misleading.

### 🟠 High

- [x] 1. [`./limesium-reactive-logging/src/test/kotlin/eu/inqudium/limesium/reactive/logging/RequestLoggingAutoConfigurationTest.kt:91` (representative; 19 methods without any marker, 21 with a partial set)] {High} {Confidence: high} {Test style} Test methods without the mandated Given/When/Then markers
  - Actual pattern: 19 of 99 `@Test` methods carry no `// Given` / `// When` / `// Then` comment at all (counting basis: `awk` over every `@Test` body, including the combined `// Given/When` and `// When/Then` forms): eight of thirteen in `RequestLoggingAutoConfigurationTest`, all four in `TwinContractTest`, three of five in `TraceparentTest`, one each in `HandlerMappingAttributeTest`, `RequestLoggingPropertiesTest`, `EndpointLogFieldTest`, `RequestLoggingWebFilterBodyAndHeaderTest`. A further 21 methods carry one or two of the three markers — the Spring integration classes (When/Then without Given) and several single-assertion pins. Many of the bare methods do carry the three-question preamble (*What is tested / Success criteria / Why it matters*), which explains intent but is not the block structure the convention asks for.
  - Reference: `./CLAUDE.md`, "Testing conventions": "Given / When / Then structure, with comments marking each block … must follow this format without exception."
  - Idiomatic alternative: add the markers even where a block is trivial — the house form `// Given/When/Then: …` on one line (used in `TraceparentTest` itself for two of its five tests) suffices for pins; for context-runner tests, `// Given: <the runner configuration>` before `.run { … }` and `// Then:` inside the lambda.
  - Effect: consistency with a non-negotiable convention; the auto-configuration class is the one a host maintainer reads to understand variant selection, so structure matters most there.

## 6. Systemic patterns

1. **Given/When/Then gaps — 19 of 99 methods without any marker, 21 with a partial set (counting basis: `awk` per `@Test` body).** Concentrated in `RequestLoggingAutoConfigurationTest` (8/13), `TwinContractTest` (4/4), `TraceparentTest` (3/5) and the two Spring integration classes (When/Then only). Consolidated in finding 1.
2. **Inline fully-qualified names in tests — ~24 sites in 4 classes (counting basis: `grep` for package-qualified references outside imports).** Heaviest in `RequestLoggingWebFilterMetricsTest` (~14). Consolidated in finding 2.
3. **Ambiguous analysis cross-references in KDoc — 21 occurrences in `src/main`, three formats.** Representative: `ExchangeLifecycle.kt:88`, `:226`; `CapturingDecorators.kt:20`, `:45`, `:62`; `RequestLoggingWebFilter.kt:28`, `:77`. Consolidated in finding 4; the twin has 26.
4. **Java-style definite assignment and `var` builder chains in the emitter — mirrored 1:1 in the servlet twin.** Consolidated in findings 3 and 5; fix both twins together per the README's duplication decision.

**Noted, deliberately not findings:** the early `return` inside `lock.withLock { }` in `BoundedBodyCapture` (non-local return from an inline lambda is the idiomatic Kotlin form for a guarded critical section, and the block bodies were chosen to satisfy the Kotlin 2.5 deprecation); the `suspend` filter's `catch (e: Throwable)` with rethrow (structured-concurrency idiom, documented in the KDoc); the per-class duplication of `keyValues` helpers (CLAUDE.md's independent-test-class convention); `@Volatile var` data on `Exchange` alongside the single `AtomicReference` state; the `var handlerMdc`/`chainRan` probes in tests (the idiomatic way to observe a side effect from a lambda without a mock library).
