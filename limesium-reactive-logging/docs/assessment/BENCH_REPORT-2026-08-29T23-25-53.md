## 1. Executive summary

Four findings of the input analysis were measured; **two confirmed with measured magnitude (#1, #2 — both allocation-dominant), one refuted (#3), one confirmed as a mechanism but retired as an action item at the assumed operating point (#4)**. The single most important result is the refutation of #3: the per-emission Micrometer meter re-resolution costs ~37 ns and 288 B/op and is **flat across registry size** (10 → 5 000 meters), missing both pre-fixed confirmation thresholds — the suggested meter cache would buy nothing worth its complexity. The confirmed findings are real but small at the assumed load: `select()` wastes ~0.9 KB/op and ~150 ns/op against a precomputed-sets variant at the 8-header configuration, and `mask()` is dominated by its `String.format` hex path — 3.3 KB/op and 5–8× the time of a byte-identical `HexFormat` variant — yet both translate to well under 0.1 % CPU and single-digit MB/s allocation at 500 req/s: worth taking as trivial, zero-risk cleanups in both twins, not as a performance initiative. The `SecureRandom` probe (#4) showed textbook negative scaling — aggregate throughput at 16 threads is 4.8× LOWER than single-threaded — so the contention mechanism is real, but the uncontended path sustains ~8.6 M ids/s where the assumed operating point needs 250/s: four orders of magnitude of headroom, no action needed short of an extreme edge-facing deployment. Generalization caveat: times were measured on JDK 26 (target is 21) on a developer workstation — ratios and allocation counts carry; absolute nanoseconds do not.

## 4. Results per finding

### Finding #1 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-31-30 — Confirmed

- Claim under test: `HeaderLogProperties.select` rebuilds the lowercased exclude/mask sets and intermediate collections per call although the configuration is immutable (allocation, 2× per request when header logging is configured).
- Instrument: JMH average time + `-prof gc` — per-op CPU/allocation, isolatable unit; allocation is the primary axis.
- Baseline vs. candidate: production `select()` vs. `PrecomputedHeaderSelect` (benchmark-local, output-equality asserted in setup; masking empty in all configs to keep #2's cost out).
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): confirmed at ≥ 200 ns/op OR ≥ 500 B/op delta at a realistic configuration; retired otherwise.
- Result (mean ± 99.9 % CI, 24 iterations):

  | Config | Baseline | Candidate | Δ time | Δ alloc |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | EXPLICIT_2 | 69.3 ± 1.0 ns, 384 B/op | 43.1 ± 0.9 ns, 216 B/op | 26 ns | 168 B/op |
  | EXPLICIT_8_EXCL_4 | 307.4 ± 7.0 ns, 1 496 ± 27 B/op | 157.9 ± 1.9 ns, 608 B/op | 150 ns | **888 B/op** |
  | WILDCARD_EXCL_4 | 463.0 ± 14.3 ns, 2 451 ± 77 B/op | 286.0 ± 3.3 ns, 1 520 B/op | 177 ns | **931 B/op** |

- Fork variance: stable (CIs ≤ 3 % of mean throughout).
- System translation: 2 calls/request × 500 req/s at the 8-header config ≈ 0.9 MB/s allocation saved and ~0.15 ms/s CPU (≈ 0.015 % of one core). Real, measured, small.
- Verdict: **Confirmed** — the ≥ 500 B/op gate is met at both realistic configs (misses both gates at the minimal 2-header config). New evidence class `Mechanism: established`/`Effect: measured`; priority recomputed **P3**: apply as a trivial, zero-risk cleanup (precompute in `init`), in BOTH twins (`HeaderLogProperties` is a twin-contract class), but do not expect a visible latency effect.
- Change cost: minimal — private derivation from immutable state, no API or output change; lockstep change in the servlet twin required.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/header-select.txt` (+ `.json`)

### Finding #2 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-31-30 — Confirmed (allocation axis; the CPU claim retires at the operating point)

- Claim under test: `mask()` pays a per-value `MessageDigest.getInstance` lookup and eight `String.format("%02x")` calls (CPU-typed in the input analysis).
- Instrument: JMH average time + `-prof gc`; inputs rotate through a 128-value pool to defeat constant folding; output equality of all three implementations asserted in setup.
- Baseline vs. candidates: production `mask()` vs. (a) `HexFormat` rendering with per-call `getInstance`, (b) additionally a thread-local digest.
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): confirmed at ≥ 2× candidate time or ≥ 500 B/op over; retired regardless of ratio if baseline < 500 ns/op absolute.
- Result (mean ± CI):

  | Length | Baseline | HexFormat | Cached digest |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 8 | 432.0 ± 7.1 ns, 3 280 ± 15 B/op | 87.4 ± 0.4 ns, 424 B/op | 55.3 ± 0.6 ns, 168 B/op |
  | 64 | 504.9 ± 18.1 ns, 3 456 ± 67 B/op | 116.9 ± 2.4 ns, 488 ± 9 B/op | 83.5 ± 0.6 ns, 224 B/op |
  | 512 | 686.6 ± 14.7 ns, 3 840 ± 62 B/op | 299.8 ± 4.0 ns, 936 ± 9 B/op | 271.6 ± 2.9 ns, 672 B/op |

- Fork variance: stable (CIs ≤ 4 %).
- Rule tension, resolved openly: both branches of the pre-fixed rule fire — the confirm branch (4–5× time, ~2 900 B/op over) at every length, the retire branch (< 500 ns absolute) at length 8 and marginally at 64. Reading the rule's intent (the absolute floor guards against optimizing a system-negligible op), the finding is **confirmed as an allocation finding and retired as a latency concern**: the dominant cost is measurably the `%02x` path (~2 900 of ~3 300 B/op vanish with `HexFormat` alone; the thread-local digest adds only ~30 ns and ~250 B more).
- System translation: 1 000 masks/s (assumed) × ~2.9 KB ≈ 2.9 MB/s allocation saved; CPU ≈ 0.35 ms/s (0.035 % of one core). Small.
- Verdict: **Confirmed**, `Effect: measured`; cost type corrected CPU → allocation-dominant; priority **P3**: take the `HexFormat` variant (byte-identical output — the fingerprint is a twin contract, and the equality gate in the benchmark proves it), skip the thread-local digest (its extra complexity buys ~30 ns).
- Change cost: minimal for the `HexFormat` step; the thread-local variant adds lifecycle/ownership questions and is not recommended.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/mask.txt` (+ `.json`)

### Finding #3 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-31-30 — Refuted

- Claim under test: re-resolving the body meters through `Counter.builder(...).register(registry)` per emission (builder + `Meter.Id` + `Tags` + registry lookup) is a meaningful per-request cost, growing with host-registry size.
- Instrument: JMH average time + `-prof gc`, against the REAL `EndpointLoggingMetrics` (bytecode-public) in steady state; registry pre-populated with 10/500/5 000 unrelated meters.
- Baseline vs. candidate: production `requestBodyRead(template, COMPLETE)` vs. increment through a once-resolved `Counter` reference (same `Meter.Id`).
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): confirmed at ≥ 300 ns/op or ≥ 300 B/op over the cached reference; retired otherwise.
- Result (mean ± CI):

  | Registry size | Baseline | Cached counter | Δ time | Δ alloc |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | 39.2 ± 1.1 ns, 269 ± 21 B/op | 3.91 ± 0.02 ns, ≈ 0 B/op | 35 ns | 269 B/op |
  | 500 | 36.9 ± 1.4 ns, 288 B/op | 3.96 ± 0.11 ns, ≈ 0 B/op | 33 ns | 288 B/op |
  | 5 000 | 37.7 ± 0.8 ns, 288 B/op | 3.92 ± 0.04 ns, ≈ 0 B/op | 34 ns | 288 B/op |

- Fork variance: stable.
- System translation: ≤ 3 meters × 500 req/s × ~288 B ≈ 0.43 MB/s and ~0.005 % of one core — immaterial.
- Verdict: **Refuted** — both thresholds missed (35 ns ≪ 300 ns; 269–288 B < 300 B), and the input analysis's scaling concern ("lookup cost additionally grows with total meter count") is directly disproven: the lookup is hash-based O(1), flat from 10 to 5 000 meters. The proposed meter cache would add a concurrent map and lifecycle coupling to save ~35 ns/request. Likely reason the static estimate overshot: the builder/Id/Tags construction is cheaper than assumed and partially scalar-replaced.
- Change cost of the retired candidate: not applicable — recommendation is to leave the production code as is.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/body-meter.txt` (+ `.json`)

### Finding #4 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-31-30 — Confirmed mechanism, retired as an action item at the operating point

- Claim under test: `UUID.randomUUID()`'s process-global `SecureRandom` serializes event-loop threads for header-less traffic (cost type wait; effect unknown).
- Instrument: JMH throughput at `-t 1` and `-t 16` as a contention PROBE (the input plan's JFR-under-load measurement remains the instrument for real event-loop stalls; this probe bounds mechanism and magnitude).
- Baseline vs. candidate: `UUID.randomUUID().toString()` vs. a `ThreadLocalRandom`-based v4-format id (the input report's documented host escape hatch; not cryptographically random).
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): contention confirmed if per-thread baseline throughput drops > 5× from 1 to 16 threads while the candidate scales.
- Result (mean ± CI, aggregate throughput):

  | Threads | Baseline | Candidate |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 8.60 ± 0.17 ops/µs (≈ 116 ns/op, 187 ± 12 B/op) | 142.1 ± 2.1 ops/µs (≈ 7 ns/op, 80 B/op) |
  | 16 | **1.80 ± 0.06 ops/µs** (aggregate LOWER than 1 thread) | 422.9 ± 3.8 ops/µs |

  Baseline per-thread throughput drops ~76× (8.60 → 0.113 ops/µs); the aggregate at 16 threads is 4.8× BELOW the single-threaded aggregate — negative scaling, the signature of a lock convoy. The candidate scales positively (3× aggregate).
- Fork variance: stable (worst CI ±3.6 %).
- System translation — the decisive step: the assumed operating point needs ~250 generated ids/s; the fully serialized baseline sustains ~8.6 M/s. That is **more than four orders of magnitude of headroom**; even at 100× the assumed load the draw rate uses < 0.3 % of the serialized capacity, and the uncontended 116 ns/op confirms the input's `100 ns - 10 µs` cost class at its lower edge.
- Verdict: **mechanism Confirmed** (`Effect: measured` for the probe), **finding retired as an action item** at the assumed operating point — new priority **P4**: change nothing; keep the input report's note that the injectable `CorrelationIdGenerator` is the escape hatch for extreme edge-facing hosts (≥ tens of thousands of header-less req/s), and let the production `endpoint.logging.correlation.id{source=generated}` counter size the real exposure before anyone acts. The JFR-under-load measurement is only warranted if such a host exists.
- Change cost of the candidate: zero in-module (bean swap by the host); the host trades cryptographic randomness of ids — acceptable for correlation ids but a deliberate host decision.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/correlation-t1.txt`, `./benchmarks/results/correlation-t16.txt` (+ `.json`)
