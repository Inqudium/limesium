## 1. Executive summary

The comment base of this module carries far more knowledge than dead weight. Production code is 2,197 lines of which 853 (39 %) are comment lines, and almost all of that stock is why/why-not knowledge: fail-open boundaries, the commit/terminal race, pooled-buffer ownership, the propagation-mode prerequisite, twin-parity decisions. Ceremony is practically absent - there is no `@param x the x`, no commented-out code, no TODO at all, no change history, no separator banner. The module also has an unusually strong anchoring habit: 23 production comments and 22 test comments cite a specific finding of a specific, timestamped, immutable analysis document, which makes most of the why-knowledge traceable rather than folklore.

The debt sits in three places. First, **drift that has already happened**: the module was derived from the servlet twin, and servlet vocabulary survived in reactive documentation - the metrics class announces "five meters" while it registers six names, and the open-exchanges gauge KDoc tells an operator to look for `requestDestroyed`, a servlet listener that does not exist in this stack. Second, **cross-module claims enforced only by prose**: "identical message format to the servlet twin" and "keep `Traceparent` in lockstep with web-client" are maintenance rules nobody's build checks, unlike the field names and the masking fingerprint, which ARE locked by tests. Third, **scattered concept knowledge**: the propagation-mode prerequisite (`limited` vs. `auto`) is explained in four places, and the enum KDoc of `EndpointLogField` is a near-verbatim copy of the servlet twin's that has already diverged in three spots.

Because no documentation tool is configured, every `[Symbol]` link is unchecked; today they all resolve (manually verified), but nothing will say so tomorrow. The anchor hub of the module is the analysis-report chain rather than ADRs - the reports are immutable, which is good for traceability, but they are by their own rule "read-only history", so decisions are never restated as rules anywhere canonical.

**Conclusion.**

1. *State:* more knowledge than dead weight; center of gravity of the debt is **drift** (copy-paste residue from the servlet twin, ~6 locations) and **prose-only lockstep rules** (2), followed by concept scattering. Roughly 15-20 % of the production comment lines can silently become wrong - the cross-module claims, the propagation-mode paragraphs and the twin-copied enum KDoc; the rest is either location-bound why-knowledge or pinned by a test.
2. *Reducibility:* **condensation** potential is modest - around 8-10 % of production comment lines (double substantiation in `ExchangeLifecycle`, proof-keeping in the emitter and `TraceMdcKeys`, the `EndpointLogField` copy). **Decompositions** are not needed: every concept block already sits in a KDoc, not between statements. The limiting factor is the absence of a canonical location for decisions (no ADR directory) - without it, every "remove the derivation, keep the rule" recommendation has nowhere to point except the immutable report chain.
3. *Essential risks:*
   - 📚🔴 nowhere - no finding deletes an only copy; the report chain holds every derivation.
   - The two Critical findings (`five meters`, `requestDestroyed`) mislead an operator reading meter documentation during an incident.
   - The `Traceparent`/web-client lockstep is a rule with no enforcement; the twin already drifted once (version/flags) and was re-aligned by hand.
   - Unchecked symbolic references everywhere (no Dokka) - today correct, tomorrow unknown.
