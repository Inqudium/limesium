## 1. Executive summary

This module's comment base is the ORIGINAL of the twin pair - the reactive module was derived from it - and it shows the same profile: 835 comment lines on 2,199 production lines (38 %), almost entirely why/why-not knowledge (servlet-spec subtleties of `reset`/`sendError`, the async marking-versus-emission split, the load-bearing filter order, the fail-open stages), with no ceremony, no commented-out code, no TODO, no change history. 22 production and 24 test comments anchor their rationale on a specific finding of a specific immutable analysis report, and the module has three genuinely tool-backed cross-module contracts (`EndpointLogFieldTest` against the shipped ELK template, `EndpointLoggingReferenceConfigTest` against the reference YAML, `TwinContractTest` literals).

The debt is concentrated in three places. First, **drift that has already happened** - three Critical findings: the `EndpointLogField` KDoc refers to a helper "named [checked]" that is called `format` (an unresolved symbolic reference nobody's build catches - the same defect exists in the reactive twin and was missed by today's reactive audit, see section 2); the public `slowRequestThreshold` KDoc still explains the OLD millisecond-truncating comparison although the emitter compares at full precision since the 16-49-01 remediation; and `EndpointLoggingMetrics` announces "five meters" while registering six. Second, **the same statement twice within one file or across two files**: `CapturingResponseWrapper` states three of its rules in the class KDoc and again at the override; the async body-capture boundary and the `DeferredResult` boundary each live in two class KDocs. Third, **bare references**: eight test comments cite "(assessment finding N)" without naming one of the six assessments, and two production comments point at a private function in web-client and at "the analogous clock reasoning in resilience-observation" without carrying the reasoning.

The highest-value comment in the module - the `+ 10` filter-order KDoc in the auto-configuration - is also the one that mixes rule and proof most visibly: the rule (the observation filter must wrap this one, or the trace join silently disappears) is load-bearing and pinned by `RequestLoggingFilterTracingIntegrationTest`; the proof (a constant read from the bytecode of a named Boot jar version) is derivation that drifts with every Boot upgrade.

**Conclusion.**

1. *State:* more knowledge than dead weight; center of gravity of the debt is **drift** (3 Critical, all copy-or-history residue) and **repetition** (3 within-file or cross-file duplicates of load-bearing statements), then bare references. Roughly 15 % of the production comment lines can silently become wrong: the twin-copied `EndpointLogField` prose, the version-pinned derivations, the cross-module textual references, and the three already-drifted sites.
2. *Reducibility:* **condensation** potential around 8-10 % of production comment lines (the within-file repeats in `CapturingResponseWrapper`, the derivation halves in the auto-configuration and emitter, the duplicated boundary paragraphs). **Decompositions** are not needed - the largest inline comment block in `RequestLoggingFilter` is five lines; every concept paragraph already sits in a KDoc. Limiting factor, as in the twin: no canonical decision record (no ADR directory), so "keep the rule, move the derivation" has only the immutable report chain to point at.
3. *Essential risks:*
   - 📚🔴 nowhere - no finding deletes an only copy.
   - Finding 2 misleads a host tuning `slow-request-threshold` on the public configuration surface.
   - Finding 1 proves that symbolic references in this repository drift unnoticed; without a documentation run there is no zero to reach on "unresolved references".
   - The `+ 10` order comment must never be reduced below its rule - it is the only place that says moving the order strips the trace join.
