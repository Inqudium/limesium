## 1. Executive summary

Two servlet-specific findings were measured, two were spot-checked for twin inheritance, and two verdicts were inherited from the reactive session. **#1 confirmed:** the unconditional `PathContainer.parsePath` costs 49–201 ns and **344–1 343 B/op** depending on segment count — the ≥ 300 B/op gate is met already at a 3-segment path, and with the shipped defaults every byte of it is spent on a result that is discarded; the sketched one-condition short-circuit removes it entirely. **#5 refuted as a P2 concern:** the writer-path double encoding is real and behaves exactly as described — linear in body size, identical cost in count-only and capped mode, so the full body IS encoded twice — but the measured magnitude is ~1.7 µs per 100 KB and ~18 µs per 1 MB of ASCII against a pre-fixed 20-µs-at-100-KB gate: the JDK's vectorized ASCII encoding makes the second pass an order of magnitude cheaper than the static analysis feared, with zero steady-state allocation. The twin spot checks came in within 2–3 % of the reactive numbers (select 313 vs 307 ns, identical 1 496 B/op; mask 519 vs 505 ns, 3 464 vs 3 456 B/op), so the reactive session's verdicts transfer: **#2 and #3 confirmed** (small, measured, P3 cleanups in the shared class), **#4 refuted** (no meter cache), **#6 retired** at the operating point. Net optimization queue for this module: the #1 short-circuit plus the shared `HeaderLogProperties` cleanup — nothing else earned a change. Generalization caveat as before: JDK 26 on a workstation — ratios and bytes carry, absolute nanoseconds do not; for #5 the ASCII fast path additionally makes the measured overhead a LOWER bound for multi-byte content.

## 4. Results per finding

### Finding #1 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-49-04 — Confirmed

- Claim under test: `shouldNotFilter` parses the request URI into a `PathContainer` on every dispatch even when no include patterns and no exclude prefixes are configured (the shipped default), discarding the result unused; async exchanges pay twice.
- Instrument: JMH average time + `-prof gc`. `shouldNotFilter` is protected on a final class, so the unit under measurement is the mechanism itself: `PathContainer.parsePath(uri)` (baseline) vs. the sketched empty-config short-circuit (candidate, the constant path). URIs rotate through a 64-entry pool.
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): confirmed at ≥ 150 ns/op or ≥ 300 B/op at a typical segment count; retired otherwise.
- Result (mean ± 99.9 % CI, 24 iterations):

  | Segments | Baseline parse | Candidate short-circuit |
  |---|---|---|
  | 3 | 49.2 ± 1.4 ns, **344 B/op** | 0.49 ± 0.01 ns, ≈ 0 B/op |
  | 7 | 113.6 ± 2.8 ns, **712 B/op** | 0.49 ± 0.01 ns, ≈ 0 B/op |
  | 12 | 201.0 ± 3.0 ns, **1 342 B/op** | 0.49 ± 0.01 ns, ≈ 0 B/op |

- Fork variance: stable (CIs ≤ 3 %).
- System translation: 500 req/s × 344–712 B ≈ 0.17–0.36 MB/s allocation and ≤ 0.006 % of one core — small in absolute terms, but purchased for nothing under the shipped defaults, and the input analysis's per-dispatch/segment-linear claims are both confirmed (near-perfectly linear in segment count).
- Verdict: **Confirmed** — the ≥ 300 B/op gate is met at every tested path shape (the ≥ 150 ns gate additionally from 7 segments up). New evidence class `Mechanism: established`/`Effect: measured`; priority stays **P3** by system effect: apply the one-condition short-circuit (return before parsing when both lists are empty) as a trivial, zero-risk cleanup; configured-pattern semantics untouched.
- Change cost: minimal — a guard clause; the decode-per-segment security semantics for CONFIGURED patterns is not touched.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/path-activation.txt` (+ `.json`)

### Finding #5 from PERF_ANALYSIS-2026-08-29T22-49-04 — Refuted (as a material cost; the mechanism is confirmed as described)

- Claim under test: every character written through `getWriter` is encoded a second time by the capture encoder — past the cap and in count-only mode — a cost linear in body size that could matter for template-rendered responses with `measure-response-body-size` on.
- Instrument: JMH average time + `-prof gc` against the REAL `CapturingResponseWrapper` over a `MockHttpServletResponse` subclass whose writer is a null sink; one operation = one response body written in 8 KiB ASCII chunks (the loop is the operation, not amplification). Three paths: plain delegate writer (baseline), wrapper in count-only mode (`BoundedBodyCapture(0)`), wrapper at the default 16 KiB cap.
- Decision rule (fixed before the run): confirmed if the wrapped path adds ≥ 20 µs at the 100 KB body or scales super-linearly; retired if under 20 µs at 100 KB.
- Result (mean ± CI):

  | Body | Baseline | Count-only | Capped 16 KiB |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 10 KB | 0.009 ± 0.001 µs | 0.153 ± 0.002 µs | 0.152 ± 0.002 µs |
  | 100 KB | 0.102 ± 0.001 µs | 1.827 ± 0.037 µs | 1.819 ± 0.041 µs |
  | 1 000 KB | 1.075 ± 0.032 µs | 18.91 ± 0.31 µs | 19.29 ± 0.64 µs |

  Steady-state allocation ≈ 0 B/op on every path (the encoder reuses its buffers) — there is no allocation dimension to this finding at all.
- Fork variance: stable (worst CI ± 3.3 %).
- Mechanism check: confirmed exactly as the analysis described it — scaling is linear (10× body → 10× cost, at every step), and count-only equals capped within noise, proving the full body runs through the encoder regardless of the cap. The MAGNITUDE is what the static analysis could not know: the JDK's vectorized ASCII encode moves ~55 GB/s, so the second pass costs ~1.7 µs per 100 KB.
- System translation: ~1.7 µs per 100 KB template response ≈ 0.2 % of an assumed 1 ms render — an order of magnitude under the pre-fixed gate. ASCII caveat: multi-byte-heavy content leaves the SIMD path; even a conservative 5× factor stays under the gate at 100 KB (~9 µs). Only multi-MB writer-rendered responses would reach tens of µs — and those have larger problems than the tee.
- Verdict: **Refuted** as a P2 concern — retired. `Mechanism: established` + `Effect: measured (negligible)`; new priority **P4: leave unchanged** — the exact-byte-count metric contract stays, and the input report's optimization sketch (approximate counting past the cap) is explicitly NOT recommended: it would trade metric exactness for ~2 µs per 100 KB. Likely reason the estimate overshot: the analysis assumed scalar charset-encoding cost; the fast path is vectorized.
- Change cost: not applicable — no change recommended.
- Raw output: `./benchmarks/results/writer-capture.txt` (+ `.json`)

## 7. Side observations (unmeasured, candidates for a future analysis)

- The JDK's ASCII charset-encoding fast path moves ~55 GB/s on this machine — static analyses should stop pricing per-char encoding as scalar work. A multi-byte-heavy (`ä`/CJK) variant of `WriterCaptureBenchmark` would bound the non-ASCII factor; deliberately not run here since even a 5× factor clears the gate.
- `PathContainer.parsePath` allocation is almost exactly linear at ~110 B per segment (344/712/1 342 B for 3/7/12) — a useful calibration constant for future per-dispatch estimates.
- The null-sink baseline (`PrintWriter` over `Writer.nullWriter()`) is largely optimized away (9 ns per 10 KB); any future wrapper benchmark should treat baseline-relative percentages with suspicion and use absolute deltas, as done here.
